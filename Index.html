The halt, announced hours before the American delegation landed at Vnukovo, is the first pause in long-range attacks on the Ukrainian capital since the spring. Kyiv says it is waiting to see whether the order holds through the night.

President Vladimir Putin has ordered a 72-hour pause in long-range strikes on Kyiv, the Kremlin said on Saturday, hours before two envoys sent by President Donald Trump arrived in Moscow for a fresh round of talks aimed at ending the war in Ukraine. The order covers missile and drone attacks on the capital and its surrounding region, and takes effect from midnight local time.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov described the measure as a gesture made “in the interest of the negotiations” and declined to say whether it would be extended. Ukrainian officials responded cautiously, noting that previous announced pauses had not covered front-line areas in the east, where fighting continued through the morning.

“We will judge the pause by what happens tonight, not by what was said this morning.”
